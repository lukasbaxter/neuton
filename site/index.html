What you get

Everything rebuilt, nothing inherited.

Starting from zero is only worth it if the result is genuinely different. Here is what that buys.

⚡

Boots instantly

The goal is click to title screen with no perceptible delay. Not "faster than vanilla" — fast enough that the loading step stops existing.

🎯

Frames, not excuses

A renderer designed for modern GPUs from the first line, rather than a decade of compatibility layers stacked on immediate-mode OpenGL.

🌄

Shader packs

Shader support is part of the renderer's design rather than something bolted on afterwards by intercepting the game's draw calls.

🌐

Servers only, on purpose

No world generation, no save format, no integrated server. Cutting everything single-player removes an enormous amount of work that never draws a frame.

👥

Multiple accounts

Shared machines, alts, a second player on the same computer. Sign in as many accounts as you like and switch between them instantly.

🔍

Nothing hidden

Every line is public. No telemetry, no analytics, no account on our side, no server of ours between you and the game.

How it works

The trick is doing the work early.

Most of what Minecraft does at startup produces exactly the same answer every single launch: parsing 1,199 block state files, baking 2,658 block models, building registries, stitching texture atlases, compiling shaders. neuton does all of it once, when the client is built, and ships the result.

Startup workNormallyIn neuton
Registries & block statesParsed from JSON at launchStatic tables in the binary
Packet definitionsBuilt by reflectionCompile-time constants
Block modelsParsed, then bakedPre-baked to geometry
Texture atlasStitched every launchPre-stitched, GPU-ready
ShadersCompiled every launchCached pipelines

What is left at startup is little more than mapping a few files into memory and drawing. The data itself still comes from the real game — neuton reads your own legitimate Minecraft installation to generate those tables, so it stays exactly in step with the version it targets.

Your account

What neuton does with your sign-in.

Short version: it signs you in with Microsoft, keeps the result on your own computer, and sends it nowhere else.

How you sign in
Microsoft's official device code flow. neuton shows a code, you enter it on Microsoft's own website, and approve there.
Your password
Never seen, never typed into neuton, never stored. You only ever enter it on Microsoft's site.
What is stored
One file on your machine holding your sign-in, readable only by your user account. Nothing is uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to.
Who it talks to
Microsoft, Xbox Live and Mojang to sign in, and whichever game server you choose to join. Nothing else, ever.
Telemetry
None. No analytics, no crash reporting, no usage data, no accounts on our side. There is no "our side".
Signing out
Deletes the file. You can also revoke neuton's access from your Microsoft account at account.live.com/consent/Manage.
